# Design decision log

Use one entry per material product, UX, visual, architecture, asset, or interaction decision. Keep it lightweight; omit irrelevant fields. Store it where the team can actually find and update it.

## Decision [ID] — [short title]

- **Date / version:**
- **Owner / reviewer:**
- **Surface, route, component, or token affected:**
- **Status:** Proposed / accepted / rejected / superseded
- **User and product objective:** What user task or product outcome does this support?
- **Evidence:** Link research, user feedback, analytics, policy, existing system, or technical constraint. Separate observation from interpretation.
- **Assumptions / confidence:** What is not yet known?
- **Options considered:** Include meaningful alternatives, including the status quo where relevant.
- **Selection criteria:** User clarity, accessibility, brand fit, performance, localization, technical feasibility, maintainability, rights, cost, etc. Choose only relevant criteria.
- **Decision and rationale:** Explain why this choice fits this context. Make the rationale inspectable, not an adjective such as “premium.”
- **Trade-offs and risks:** Include people or contexts that may be disadvantaged.
- **Implementation contract:** Relevant tokens, component, content, interaction states, framework, dependency, or asset source/license.
- **Validation plan:** What evidence would confirm or falsify the decision? Include method, task, viewport/platform, and success/failure signals.
- **Validation result:** Record what actually happened, with links to screenshots, test reports, user notes, or issues. State what was not tested.
- **Follow-up / revisit trigger:** Date or condition that should reopen the decision.

## Rejected alternatives

For any rejected direction worth preserving, record the option and the reason it was not selected. This prevents the same alternatives from being re-litigated without context and avoids confusing “not chosen” with “universally bad.”
